About

Gakjab Salim · About this site

Gakjab Salim is a site that collects genuinely useful free web tools for everyday life and work, one at a time. We build tools around one question — "does this actually help someone make a real decision?" — rather than piling on flashy features. We currently run the Salary Negotiation Calculator, the Room Furniture Layout Simulator, the Resignation Timing Calculator, the Couple Referee, the Minecraft Seed Recommender, the Keyword Checker, the Fishing Tide Guide, and Map Archery.

Who makes this

Gakjab Salim was started by someone who used to build internal tools for a company. After years of writing calculators and small automations that only ran on an office network, it became obvious that plenty of people outside that network were doing the same work by hand. This site began as an attempt to move those tools somewhere anyone could reach them.

That's also why tools get picked for being things people actually do manually rather than things with high search volume — rebuilding a spreadsheet before a salary negotiation, standing in an empty room with a tape measure, looking up tide tables before a fishing trip.

It's built and run by one person. That means fixes ship quickly, but things also get missed. If you find an error, tell us through the contact page and it'll get looked at — several of the tools running here started from exactly that kind of message.

How we decide what to build

Before building a tool we ask three things. First, are people doing this by hand right now? If it takes a fresh spreadsheet or several searches to get an answer, it's worth building. Second, does it give an answer? Calculators that just emit a number are common, so we try to also judge whether that number is good or bad for you. Third, are we honest about what it can't do? Every tool page carries a "what this can't do" section, and we state data limits and margins of error up front. Setting accurate expectations beats manufacturing false confidence.

That principle also explains what we deliberately left out: no sign-ups, no saved results, no paid tiers. Most tools compute entirely inside your browser and never send your input to a server. For the ones that genuinely need outside processing — the Couple Referee's AI ruling, the Fishing Tide Guide's tide lookup — we spell out what goes where, both on screen and in our privacy policy.

Salary Negotiation Calculator

The Salary Negotiation Calculator answers the two questions job-seekers care about most when facing a job change or salary negotiation — "is this offer fair?" and "how much does my take-home pay actually change?" — on a single screen.

Resignation Timing Calculator

The Resignation Timing Calculator uses your start date to compute the date your severance pay kicks in and the date your next batch of paid leave accrues, then recommends a resignation date that captures both. Below that is a quick click-through self-check on why you're considering leaving (a reference tool, not a substitute for professional advice).

Couple Referee

The Couple Referee is a for-fun reference tool: each partner writes their side of a dispute, and five judges with different personalities each rule based on their own criteria. Rulings use AI — both partners' written answers are sent to an AI model provider for judging (not stored, discarded right after judging). If the AI can't be reached, it automatically falls back to a rule-based ruling using only the multiple-choice answers, and the result screen shows which method was used. It's designed to only take a side when the fault is genuinely clear, and switches to support-resource guidance instead of a ruling if it detects signs of physical threats or violence.

Room Furniture Layout Simulator

The Room Furniture Layout Simulator answers the question you have before a move: "will this furniture actually fit?" Rather than just picking a floor area, you draw your room's real shape (L-shaped, with a balcony, and so on), then place a bed, desk, or fridge and get a live check on whether enough passage width is left for a person to walk through and whether doors and drawers have room to open. Fitting a piece of furniture into a room and being able to live in that room are two different things. Standard bed sizes are preloaded, so you don't have to measure them yourself.

Map Archery

Map Archery picks one of South Korea's 250 districts when you can't decide where to travel. Instead of pulling a random entry from a list, you shoot an arrow at a continuously spinning map, and wherever it lands becomes the destination. District boundaries come from Statistics Korea's SGIS service, released under Korea Open Government License Type 1.

Keyword Checker

The Keyword Checker takes a page link and ranks the SEO keywords actually present in the title, meta description, heading tags (H1–H3), and body copy, alongside candidate keywords worth adding. It's not full morphological analysis, so results are approximate, and suggested keywords are candidates only — actual search volume isn't verified. Shopping-site product links that block automated access (like Coupang) aren't supported.

Fishing Tide Guide

The Fishing Tide Guide only needs three picks — region, technique, and target species — to recommend real shore-fishing spots from a set of 1,076 points built on Korea's Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries open data. Today's tide phase number is calculated from the lunar calendar, and high/low tide times come from the Korea Hydrographic and Oceanographic Agency's tide forecast. Each spot is pre-matched to its nearest of 62 tide stations nationwide. Coordinates are approximate (100–200m of error is possible), so cross-check with the map and conditions on the ground. Note that the east coast has a small tidal range, so tide timing matters much less there.

Minecraft Seed Recommender

The Minecraft Seed Recommender picks from 5,204 seeds based on your taste. Answer four questions — edition, play style, landscape, and nearby structures — and seeds with documented features are matched and scored against your answers, while seeds without documented features yet are marked "features unconfirmed" so you can explore them yourself. Seed data is compiled from public guide sites and communities, so it's secondhand information and may differ from the actual in-game terrain.

What these tools solve

  • Where an offer sits (top X%) versus the average for the same role and experience level
  • Visualizing your salary composition — base, bonus, cash benefits, other — and how much of it is actually negotiable when changing jobs
  • Real-time take-home pay (monthly/annual) as your salary changes
  • How realistic your target salary is, and the raise you'd need to get there

Data sources and methodology

Take-home pay is calculated from Korea's four major social insurance rates (national pension, health insurance, long-term care, employment insurance) and the National Tax Service's simplified withholding tax table. This part applies published official rates directly, so given the same assumptions (dependents, tax-free cap) the result will match other correct calculators.

The average-salary baseline per role is an estimated reference line we set for each of the 22 role categories, informed by published wage statistics from the Ministry of Employment and Labor and the Korea Employment Information Service (the Wage Information System, etc.). It is not a direct copy of those statistics — it is our own figure, calibrated to the level those public sources indicate. The percentile ("position") converts your salary against that baseline; it is not the result of a full survey of actual salary distribution.

Headcount figures are real statistics from Statistics Korea's Economically Active Population Survey, mapped onto our 22 role categories. Because that mapping is at the major-category level, roles don't divide cleanly (e.g. developers, IT, and research roles all share the "professionals and related workers" category, roughly 6.25 million people).

In short — the take-home pay calculation is grounded in official rates and is reliable, while the percentile is a reference indicator based on an estimated baseline. It's good enough to get a feel for roughly where you sit, but it isn't a figure to cite as evidence in a negotiation. If you need official statistics, check the Ministry of Employment and Labor's Wage Information System directly.

Limitations

The figures these tools produce are for reference only and carry no legal or financial weight. Your actual numbers will vary based on dependents, tax-free items, and your employer's specific policies. Please confirm your own situation against official sources before making final decisions or negotiating.
