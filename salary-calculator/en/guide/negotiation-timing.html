Salary Negotiation Timing — When Should You Bring It Up?

The same ask lands differently depending on when you make it

Published 2026-08-01 · Updated 2026-08-03

In a salary negotiation, "when you say it" matters as much as "what you say." The exact same request can succeed or fail depending purely on timing.

Job-change negotiation: right after the offer is the golden window

Your leverage in a job change peaks right after you receive a formal offer, before you sign. The company has already decided to hire you, so there's the most room to adjust. Once you've signed, your leverage drops sharply.

Rather than pinning down a target number early in interviews, it's often better to say "I'd like to discuss it together based on the company's structure and my experience" and defer the specifics, then negotiate the actual number once the offer is on the table.

Internal raises: right before the review/budget cycle

Internal raises are tied to the company's review and budget cycle. Ask after the budget is already locked in and you're likely to hear "maybe next time." Bring your evidence and start the conversation right before the review season begins.

Use the moment right after a win

Right after you've shipped a major project or made a visible contribution is a good time to negotiate. Your evidence is strongest while it's still fresh.

Timing to avoid

  • When company performance is poor or there's restructuring in the air
  • Right after a mistake or a rough patch on your end
  • During a crunch or crisis when your manager has no bandwidth
