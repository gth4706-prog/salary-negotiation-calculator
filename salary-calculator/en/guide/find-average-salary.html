How to Find the Average Salary for Your Role

Using public data to find where you stand

Published 2026-08-01 · Updated 2026-08-03

"Is my salary above average or below?" Answering that question well requires a trustworthy source. Government-surveyed, publicly released data is far more accurate than forum posts or tables of unknown origin.

Reliable public sources (Korea)

  • Wage Information System (wage.go.kr) — run by the Ministry of Employment and Labor and the Korea Employment Information Service. Provides average and percentile wages by role, level, experience, industry, and company size.
  • Public Data Portal (data.go.kr) — offers datasets like "wage information by occupation" with upper/median/lower wage figures, free via file or API.
  • Statistics Korea wage statistics — useful for the big-picture trend in average wages by industry and occupation.

Why "percentile" matters more than "average"

Looking at a single average can be pulled upward by a small number of high earners and end up not matching how things actually feel. That's why it's important to look at the median (50th percentile) alongside percentiles (bottom 25% / top 25%) together.

If "the top-25% wage is $60,000," you can say your salary is "top tier" once it clears that number. Thinking in terms of where you sit within the distribution is more accurate than a single average figure.

Know the data's limitations too

Public data is usually published at the level of occupation category × experience band (e.g. 1–3 years) × percentile. You won't get an ultra-precise number for "developer, exactly 3 years," but in exchange you get the big advantage of a large sample size and a clear source.
