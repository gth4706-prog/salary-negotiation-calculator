Target Salary: A Number, or "Negotiable"?

A strategy for each application/interview situation

Published 2026-08-01 · Updated 2026-08-03

One of the fields people hesitate over most on an application or in an interview is target salary. Write too high and you worry about being screened out; write too low and it feels like leaving money on the table. The right answer depends on your situation.

When stating a specific number works better

If you already know the market average for your role and experience, state a specific number. A grounded case like "given my position relative to the average, I'm targeting this figure" is more convincing to a recruiter than a vague range or an evasive non-answer. Before you settle on a number, check your average position for your role and experience with the Salary Negotiation Calculator.

When a range or "negotiable" works better

In the early stage of an application, before you know the company's pay scale or compensation level, it can be safer to leave room with a range ("X–Y% of my current salary") or write "negotiable based on company standards." Locking in a number with too little information can end up shrinking your own leverage.

That said, writing only "negotiable" with no preparation behind it risks simply accepting whatever low number the company proposes. Even if you write "negotiable," privately decide your walk-away number ahead of time.

3 practical tips

  • Precompute a raise percentage based on your current salary so you can answer immediately if asked for a specific number in an interview.
  • Use a different strategy depending on whether target salary comes up at the application stage or right before an offer — see Negotiation Timing for details.
  • When you state a number, be clear whether it's base salary only or total compensation (base + bonus). Leaving it ambiguous lets the company interpret it in their favor.
